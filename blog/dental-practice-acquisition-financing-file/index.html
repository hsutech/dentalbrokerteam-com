How to Build a Dental Practice Acquisition Financing File

A dental acquisition file must connect the buyer, the practice and the transaction. Strong collections do not replace lease review, due diligence, transition planning or a realistic post-closing cash reserve.

Build three files that reconcile

Direct answer: organize the request into a buyer file, a practice file and a transaction file. The buyer file explains identity, clinical and management readiness, personal obligations and ownership. The practice file explains collections, expenses, patient and provider mix, lease, equipment and operations. The transaction file explains price, asset or share structure, equity, financing, working capital and transition.

The three files must tell the same story. A practice can show strong historical collections while still carrying lease risk, aging equipment, provider concentration or working-capital pressure. A qualified dentist can have excellent clinical experience while needing management support. A reasonable price can still be difficult to finance if the post-closing budget omits payroll, supplies and delayed collections.

This is general Ontario acquisition education. Financing, professional regulation, tax, privacy, employment and transaction structure require advice from qualified Canadian professionals who have reviewed the specific practice and agreement.

Show the buyer behind the corporation

Prepare identification, professional licensing information where requested, curriculum vitae, ownership structure, personal financial information, Notices of Assessment or tax records requested by the lender, and a schedule of personal obligations. If more than one dentist or investor is involved, explain roles, voting, guarantees and capital contributions.

Clinical ability and operating readiness are different questions. Describe experience with team leadership, scheduling, treatment planning, collections oversight and regulatory responsibilities without overstating skills. If a transition mentor, office manager or advisor will cover a gap, document the role and duration.

BDC notes that a business purchase financing request can require transaction details, purchase price and method, structure, letter of intent, expected closing, down payment and financial statements or returns. A dental lender may ask for additional professional and practice-specific information.

Move from gross production to transferable cash flow

Gather multiple years of financial statements and tax returns as appropriate, current year-to-date results, production and collections reports, provider-level information, hygiene performance, accounts receivable aging and a complete expense breakdown. Reconcile practice-management reports with the financial statements rather than presenting each in isolation.

Normalize carefully. Seller compensation, related-party rent, discretionary expenses and one-time costs may need analysis, but an add-back is not automatically transferable cash. Document the reason and expected post-closing treatment. Include the replacement cost of any seller work the buyer cannot or will not perform.

Test concentration. How much production depends on the selling dentist, one associate, one referral source or a narrow procedure mix? The transition plan should address retention without promising that patients, staff or revenue will remain unchanged.

Treat the lease as part of the financing

Review the remaining term, renewal options, assignment language, demolition or relocation clauses, permitted use, exclusivity, maintenance responsibilities and landlord consent. The practice may depend on a location that the financing term outlasts.

Confirm whether leasehold improvements belong to the tenant, what must be restored, and whether planned renovations require consent. Map rent increases and occupancy costs into the forecast. If real estate is purchased separately, keep the property financing and practice acquisition economics visible.

Do not assume an attractive clinic can simply move. Patient patterns, signage, accessibility, parking, equipment installation and regulatory requirements can make relocation expensive and disruptive.

Inventory equipment, technology and replacement exposure

Create a dated list of major dental units, imaging systems, sterilization equipment, compressors, suction, computers and practice-management software. Record age, ownership, liens, service history and known replacement plans. Confirm which assets are included in an asset purchase and which contracts must be assigned.

An appraisal supports a value discussion but does not replace technical inspection or cybersecurity review. Confirm software licences, data migration, backups and vendor access. Patient records involve professional and privacy obligations beyond ordinary business files.

Budget near-term capital separately from the purchase price. A practice can service acquisition debt on paper and still face stress if imaging, sterilization or IT systems require replacement soon after closing.

Fund the period after closing, not only the purchase

Build a weekly post-closing model covering payroll, laboratory bills, supplies, rent, insurance, debt payments and taxes. Consider how billing and collections behave during the ownership transition. Maintain a reserve for normal variation and verified repair risks.

Separate acquisition financing from operating availability. Ask whether working capital is included, whether a line of credit is available and when payments begin. If the buyer contributes nearly all available cash to closing, even a profitable practice can start with inadequate liquidity.

Run a case with slower collections, staff turnover and a temporary reduction in seller-generated production. The purpose is not to predict failure. It is to show what management action and reserve would be required if transition is less smooth than expected.

Compare dental financing without relying on generic claims

General searches for small business loans can help a buyer notice common questions about credit, documents and cash flow. They do not establish Canadian program eligibility or dental practice terms. Verify every claim with the lender and Canadian advisors.

Make asset, share and vendor terms explicit

The purchase agreement may be structured as an asset purchase, share purchase or another arrangement. That choice affects liabilities, contracts, tax treatment, financing and professional obligations. The lender, lawyer and accountant need to work from the same structure.

If the seller provides a vendor take-back note, record amount, payment, maturity, security, subordination and any performance condition. BDC describes vendor financing as a form of acquisition debt; its role and treatment must be reviewed in the complete capital stack.

Include adjustments for receivables, prepaid expenses, supplies, employee obligations and other closing items. Headline price alone does not show the buyer's total cash requirement or the seller's net proceeds.

Keep lender diligence and buyer diligence separate

A lender reviews the transaction to make a credit decision. The buyer still needs independent financial, legal, clinical, regulatory, employment, lease and technology diligence. Approval does not certify that the practice is fairly priced, clinically appropriate or free of operational risk.

BDC's due-diligence guidance emphasizes investigation before the purchase agreement is finalized. For a dental clinic, adapt that principle to patient-record obligations, professional rules, equipment condition, associate arrangements and continuity of care.

Maintain a diligence tracker with request, owner, due date, source and resolution. Material findings should update the price, agreement, financing file or decision to proceed. Do not let a financing deadline turn unresolved findings into assumed facts.

Use a closing checklist with a post-closing owner

Track final lender conditions, corporate documents, insurance, lease assignment, licences, purchase adjustments, lien releases, payroll setup, bank access, merchant processing, software credentials and patient communication. Identify who confirms each item and who owns it after closing.

Keep confidential records inside controlled channels. Do not place patient information in a general financing data room unless qualified advisors confirm it is necessary and handled lawfully. Use anonymized or aggregated reporting when it meets the purpose.

The financing file is complete when it supports both closing and operation. The buyer should leave the process with an executed agreement, payment schedule, working-capital plan, transition calendar and a record of assumptions to monitor during the first months.

Frequently asked questions

Does lender approval replace dental practice due diligence?

No. Lender underwriting serves the lender's credit decision. The buyer still needs independent professional diligence.

Should working capital be planned separately from purchase price?

Yes. Model payroll, supplies, laboratory bills, rent and collection timing after closing and confirm how operating liquidity will be funded.

Can a general online loan recommendation establish Canadian eligibility?

No. Verify current Canadian program rules and the specific lender's requirements for the buyer, practice and transaction.
