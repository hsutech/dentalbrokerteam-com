Which alternatives should you compare?

ROI Corporation, Henry Schein Tier Three Brokerage, Professional Practice Sales (PPS) and DentalPlace are four alternatives to investigate if you are comparing dental practice sale, purchase or valuation services in Ontario. Their public websites describe different ways to access appraisals, listings and transaction support. Choose a shortlist around the work you need, then obtain written proposals before deciding.

This guide is published by The Dental Broker Team, which operates in the same market. It is a comparison of publicly described services, not an independent review, customer satisfaction survey or ranking. The four options are not exhaustive, and their order does not indicate quality. Service descriptions were checked on November 2, 2026; current availability, fees and engagement terms require direct confirmation.

A useful comparison starts with a specific request: an appraisal for a retirement decision, a managed sale to outside buyers, help with an identified purchaser, or access to acquisition opportunities. Sending the same request to each candidate makes differences easier to see. Comparing an appraisal-only quote with a full sale mandate does not tell you which provider offers better value.

Four providers to investigate

1. ROI Corporation: start with the appraisal scope

ROI Corporation's website emphasizes practice appraisals and provides routes for buying, selling and finding practices. It also offers access to a new-listings service. That makes it a relevant candidate when your first decision is how to obtain a valuation and whether that work should lead into a sale process.

Ask which report is appropriate for your intended use and who can rely on it. Request the inputs, assumptions, delivery format and process for discussing the findings. If a sale may follow, ask whether marketing is a separate engagement and how a later change in practice performance would be handled.

A useful proposal should let you distinguish the appraisal deliverable from continuing advice. You may want a decision-making report now without committing to a sale timetable. State that clearly, so the conversation addresses the decision you actually face.

2. Henry Schein Tier Three Brokerage: examine the analysis and assigned team

Henry Schein Tier Three Brokerage presents valuation, sale and purchase services alongside listings searchable by province and practice type. Its public valuation description discusses patient metrics and cash-flow projections. These are relevant subjects to ask about when comparing the analytical content of an engagement.

Request an explanation of the information needed, the assumptions used and how the report would help with your specific decision. Ask who will gather the data, interpret the findings and manage questions from prospective purchasers. A service description does not establish which tasks are included in your particular quote.

For buyers, clarify the distinction between obtaining listing information and receiving advice on your behalf. For sellers, establish the assigned contact and communication process. Treat the answers as engagement details to confirm, rather than assuming that organizational scale determines personal attention.

3. Professional Practice Sales: compare the Ontario engagement

Professional Practice Sales' Ontario page describes practice valuation, selling and buying services and introduces its Ontario team. The site also distinguishes preliminary practice assessments from valuation services in its navigation. Those categories provide a useful starting point for asking what level of work you need.

Explain the decision the report or engagement must support. Ask whether the proposed work is an initial assessment, a fuller valuation, sale preparation or ongoing transaction management. Request the deliverables and exclusions in writing, including what happens if your plans change after the initial work.

When comparing proposals, use named responsibilities rather than general descriptions of experience. Identify who is accountable for analysis, buyer communication, offer coordination and follow-up. This makes the proposed Ontario service easier to compare with other candidates without relying on testimonials as proof of future results.

4. DentalPlace: distinguish marketplace access from professional representation

DentalPlace describes itself as an Ontario dental practice database built by a team of dental lawyers. Its public page displays listings and asks users to register to see complete details. This gives buyers another discovery channel and sellers a different service model to investigate.

Before comparing a quote with a brokerage proposal, ask exactly which marketing, negotiation, legal and closing tasks are included. Establish which professional is engaged by each party and which services require separate arrangements. A platform's legal background does not answer every representation question for a particular transaction.

Buyers should also ask how listing information is verified and updated, and what further diligence remains their responsibility. Sellers should request a description of the disclosure process and the work expected from them. Compare the complete engagement rather than assuming that a different model automatically means lower cost or less work.

Seven details that make proposals comparable

1. The decision and deliverable

Give each candidate the same brief: your role, approximate location, desired timing and the decision you need help making. List the output you expect, such as a written appraisal, a sale mandate or support evaluating a specific opportunity. Ask what is excluded. A proposal that clearly narrows its scope may be more useful than one that promises broad support without defining it.

2. The person responsible for your file

Request the day-to-day contact, the person responsible for analysis and the escalation contact. Discuss availability at the stages when you expect to need decisions. You are comparing a working arrangement, not simply a brand. Ask how responsibilities change if your original contact is unavailable or the transaction extends beyond the anticipated timetable.

3. The evidence behind the appraisal

Ask how the provider will explain adjustments, assumptions and limits. You should be able to distinguish an asking price from a valuation conclusion and understand the intended use of the report. If appraisal scope is the main uncertainty, review the questions in the Ontario valuation services comparison before requesting proposals.

4. The route from interest to qualified discussion

Ask sellers' advisors how inquiries become serious conversations and what they require before releasing additional information. Buyers can ask what registration allows them to see and what steps follow. The useful comparison is the proposed process for your file. A large public listing count alone does not tell you how relevant or ready the interested parties will be.

5. Disclosure and communication

Request a staged description of what information is shared, with whom and when. Ask how you approve public wording and how questions involving staff, patients or identifiable practice details are handled. Have your own advisors review sensitive disclosure arrangements. Avoid sharing patient-level information merely to obtain an initial service quote; start with a limited description of the engagement you need.

6. The complete cost and commitment

Ask for fees, payment triggers, outside expenses, term, cancellation provisions and obligations that may continue after termination. Request examples explaining how the quoted arrangement would apply if you already had a buyer or postponed the sale. Do not assume the lowest initial amount represents the lowest total commitment. The broker fees guide provides a separate framework for reviewing cost questions with counsel.

7. The handover between professionals

Identify who coordinates requests involving the lawyer, accountant, lender and other advisors. Ask which tasks the provider performs and which remain with you. A clear responsibility list helps expose missing work before a deadline arrives. Keep legal, tax and financing decisions with the qualified professionals responsible for your circumstances; this comparison is educational service-selection guidance.

How to turn the shortlist into a decision

Use a simple comparison sheet with one column per provider and rows for the seven details above. Record direct answers, document references and unresolved questions. Leave unknowns visibly blank rather than converting impressions into scores. If a proposal cannot be compared because its scope differs, ask for clarification before treating its price as an advantage.

For example, an owner who already has an interested associate may prioritize appraisal scope and coordination with independent advisors. An owner without a prospective buyer may place more weight on the marketing and inquiry process. These are hypothetical decision examples, not claims that any named provider is better for either case.

Check whether you are choosing a provider or reconsidering the transaction route itself. If you are deciding between a broker engagement, private sale and other ways to reach buyers, the Toronto practice-sale alternatives guide addresses that earlier question. Once the route is clear, this shortlist can help you compare actual service proposals.

Before signing, resolve important differences in scope and have the proposed agreement reviewed. If appraisal is your immediate next step, you can also review this site's appraisal service description using exactly the same criteria. Choose the engagement whose written responsibilities match your needs and whose unanswered questions have been satisfactorily resolved.

Frequently asked questions

What are alternatives to The Dental Broker Team in Ontario?

Four options to investigate are ROI Corporation, Henry Schein Tier Three Brokerage, Professional Practice Sales and DentalPlace. They describe appraisal, brokerage or marketplace services relevant to Ontario practice transactions. Confirm the scope and availability directly.

Is this an independent review of those providers?

No. This article appears on a competing provider's website and summarizes public service descriptions. It does not rate client satisfaction, claim firsthand experience or rank the firms by quality.

Which alternative charges the lowest fees?

This review does not establish a comparable fee ranking. Request written quotes covering the same work, payment triggers, additional expenses and contractual commitments before comparing total cost.

Does access to a listing mean the provider represents me?

Do not assume so. Ask whom the provider represents, what information access includes and whether separate advice or an additional engagement is needed for your purchase.

Should I contact every provider on the list?

Start with those whose published services match your immediate decision and location. Use a consistent brief and limit initial disclosure. Expand the shortlist if the proposals do not address your needs clearly.
